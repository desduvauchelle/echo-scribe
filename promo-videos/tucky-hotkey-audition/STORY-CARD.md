# Tucky — promo film story card (v1, "The Hotkey Audition")

Nothing is built yet. This is the story to agree on before I render a frame.

## The one line

**"Other apps type what you say. Tucky does something with it."** One hotkey gets you dictation, commands, notes, tasks filed by project, meetings and a second brain that answers back. It's free and runs on your Mac.

## The shape of the story

A talent-show parody. You have exactly one good hotkey left on your keyboard (the key everyone wants). Three dictation apps audition for it in front of a panel of three judges: you, your overloaded brain (the thought bubble from "Brain Tabs"), and a very tired cat.

The first two contestants are the competition, and they're **good**. That's the joke. They do one trick flawlessly, then stand there waiting for applause. The judges keep asking "…and?" Then Tucky walks on, does the same trick, and just doesn't stop. It keeps going, one capability after another, until the judges' scorecards run out of numbers.

Tone: affectionate roast, never a hit piece. The competitors are shown as polished and competent, which makes the one-trick punchline land harder and keeps us on the right side of fair. Every line about them is something they'd happily put on their own website ("turns your voice into text, fast"). Every line about Tucky is a shipped feature.

Palette and craft: a stage look. A dark stage, one spotlight, a red curtain and a cream scorecard, which turns into Tucky's cream/green UI whenever Tucky performs. The contestants are "app icons" on stage: rounded squares with their own name, drawn generically, with no copied logos. The brain bubble and the tab counter come back from "Brain Tabs" for continuity.

## Why it works

- **The competitors look good.** The joke is never "they're bad". It's "…and?" That's funnier, it's fair, and it's the only version I'd put their names on.
- **The repeated trick is the setup.** Two identical auditions train the viewer to expect a bow. When Tucky doesn't bow, that's the whole pitch, shown without a comparison table.
- **The judges carry the comedy.** Your Brain (continuity with "Brain Tabs") and the cat let the reactions do the roasting, so the narrator stays dry and never has to insult anyone.
- **The one-hotkey idea bookends the film.** It opens and closes on a single key, so "it all starts with one key" is something the viewer sees, not a claim.
