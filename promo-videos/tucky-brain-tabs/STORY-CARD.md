# Tucky — promo film story card (v1, "Brain Tabs")

Nothing is built yet. This is the story to agree on before I render a frame.

## The one line

**"Say it once. Then forget it on purpose."** Tucky lets you offload every stray thought, note and task by voice, files each one into the right project, and keeps the few things that matter today on your screen.

## The shape of the story

A day in the life of a brain with 47 tabs open. It's told from the inside and with affection: the joke is always on the brain, never on the person. The narrator is a deadpan nature-documentary voice watching "you" try to do one thing while your head keeps interrupting. The thoughts show up on screen as speech bubbles. They pop in over whatever you're doing, repeat, multiply, and loop around a tab counter in the corner ("🧠 47 tabs open").

The structural device is **the tab counter**. It climbs through the first act (47… 52… 61). Tucky brings it down, one popped bubble at a time. Then daily focus stops it from climbing back up. The film ends on a single, harmless last thought.

Arc: chaos (funny) → "you've tried systems" (the funniest, most familiar beat) → hand it off (satisfying pops) → focus (calm, with one relapse played for laughs) → a quiet end.

Palette and craft: same world as "The inquiry" (off-white paper, forest green, mint, amber, same fonts). The thought bubbles are the one new element: amber, slightly wobbly, with a soft "pop" SFX. The mascot shows up whenever Tucky takes something off your plate, and for the whole of the focus act it sits on the desktop with its "Today's focus" bubble, just like the real desktop pet.

## Why it works

- **The tab counter is the plot.** One number climbs and falls, so the viewer tracks the benefit without a single feature list or keyboard chord on screen.
- **The sticky note that says "IMPORTANT"** is the beat everyone recognizes. The humour sets up the relief, so the product never has to shout.
- **The pops are the payoff.** Every capture ends a loop you can see. That's the offloading feeling, shown instead of claimed.
- **Focus gets a relapse.** The spoon tangent is honest, and it's exactly the moment the desktop pet earns its place.
- **The joke is always on the brain, never the person**, and the film never names or diagnoses ADHD (see question 2).
