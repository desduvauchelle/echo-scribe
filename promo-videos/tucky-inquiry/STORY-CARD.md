# Tucky — promo film story card (v2, "The inquiry")

Nothing is built yet. This is the story to agree on before I render a frame.

## The one line

**"Typing is overrated. Just talk."** Tucky is the Mac app that turns your voice into text anywhere, records your meetings, and remembers your work. Free, private, runs on your Mac.

## The shape of the story

Not a feature tour. An adoption story, told as a chain of questions. It starts with one tiny habit. The habit is awkward at first. You push through, and then you can't go back. Then a question surfaces: "wait, all of this is useful, isn't it?" and that question leads to the next capability. Each answer opens the next door. The viewer should feel they are walking deeper into a house, room by room, and that they walked in on their own.

Structure: **one thing → friction → the click → the question → the next thing**, repeated four times, going deeper each time. Every chapter title on screen is a question.

The voice is inquisitive and a little wry, thinking out loud with the viewer. Present tense, second person. "You" is the hero. Tucky is the quiet thing on the menu bar that keeps answering.

Palette and craft as the LiveCase films but warmer: off-white paper, forest green, mint, amber. The mascot appears only when something clicks.

## Why it works

- **One question per chapter** keeps it an inquiry, not a pitch. The viewer asks the questions with you.
- **Friction is in the film.** Day 2 hesitates and types. That honesty is what makes "you can't go back" believable.
- **Each door opens from the inside.** Dictation → "my thoughts are worth keeping" → meetings → "what else does it know" → memory and answers → "where does it live" → privacy and price. Nothing is introduced because it's a feature. It's introduced because you asked.
- **Bookend.** Ends on the first frame, so the whole thing reads as one habit that grew.
